# Stateful retained RAW gain-path merge (`0.16.1.dev11` source)

This is an opt-in electrical/HDR building block for the Camera Simulation & Validation platform. A retained photodiode or retained-plus-trap program first evolves charge in causal row and frame time. Its **one delivered charge population** is then read through the declared HCG and LCG paths. The new RAW-only estimator merges those two original integer RAW streams into **signed delivered total charge, electron/pixel**. The original RAW, preview, state accounting and code-domain ISP replay remain available.

`camera_retained_charge_capture_program_v3` adds the merge to retained charge; `v4` adds it to retained plus one authored trap species. Both require a paired linear or nonlinear readout model and an explicit matching `gain_merge_profile`. The factory offers separate retained and trap-history draft buttons. The result contract is `camera_retained_charge_result_v3`, with `gain_merge_*` arrays for the charge estimate, per-path estimates and weights, validity, agreement, and rounding bounds. Each frame records the original HCG/LCG RAW hash, actual exposure, model-derived calibration kind, shared-population provenance and unit. Invalid or clipped paths are marked; no hidden charge-state truth is fed into the estimator.

The merge uses the model's response, covariance and actual exposure to establish a numerical rate coordinate, then returns to delivered charge with **zero dark subtraction**. It does not divide the final answer by exposure, remove residual/trap/dark charge, or recover current-exposure photocharge or scene radiance. It is **not multi-exposure HDR**. A saturated code hidden by read noise cannot be certified merely by the boundary masks. The calibration is derived from authored model parameters, not a measured camera. Physical validation is `not_established`; total predictive uncertainty is unknown.

This source is **not installed** into the primary application. The next HDR work must distinguish stateful multi-exposure radiance fusion from same-population gain fusion, then handle reset/CDS dynamics, saturation and noise tradeoffs, PWM/LFM metrics, motion and independent measurement. The prior authored whole-PWM-period AE avoided 8/4 ms oscillation but missed its 200–300 DN target in all four frames; this gain merge does not fix that control tradeoff.
